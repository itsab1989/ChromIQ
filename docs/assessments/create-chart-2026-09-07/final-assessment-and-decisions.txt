P1. Generating from "New run" stores the wrong settings into the new run
    (F-003 confirmed, cause found in R-105). Grade: OBSERVED. HIGH.
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Problem: You are on a run, choose "New run" in the bar, change a setting
(for example switch the engine off, untick "Suppress Left Clip Border",
set patch scale 1.0), press Generate. The chart is built with YOUR values.
But the new run's stored settings, and the screen a second later, show the
values from BEFORE your change (clip border suppressed, scale 0.95).

What was tested: both agents, three fresh runs, widget values read before
and after the click, the command the app actually ran, the files on disk.
The reviewer put a stack trace on the writes and named the exact path.

What was observed: the app copies a "seed block" that was captured the
moment you selected "New run" (before your edits) into the new run, then
reloads that block over your screen, twice, and finally stores it. The
run's own files disagree with each other: one record says what ran, the
other says what the block held. Pressing Generate again would (inferred,
not tried) build a different chart from the one on screen.
This is the known open item recorded as "the deeper ordering cause" of the
build-in-flight clobber; both agents now name the exact writer.

Why it matters: this is the most ordinary route in the app (new run,
change one thing, generate). It affects every per-target setting row, not
only printtarg's. Switching runs and back restores the wrong values, and
when the engine is later switched on it converts those wrong printtarg
values into its own layout, so the error travels on.

Options:
  A. Fix at the cause: when Generate creates the run, copy what is ON
     SCREEN into it, not the old block (and still clear the block).
     Pros: fixes every row at once; matches Knut's words in the design
     specification (section 4a): the block "can be modified by the user
     ... then when Generate Chart is pressed, all these settings are
     copied".
     Cons: touches the per-target seeding code; needs the two existing
     seeding drivers (11 + 74 checks) re-run. And the specification is
     not clean on this: its own rule N-3 says "Generate Chart copies IT
     (the block) into the new run", which is what the code does today, and
     the whole of section 4a is still marked "awaiting confirmation". So
     Option A also rewrites rule N-3.
  B. Re-seed the printtarg widgets from the correct record after the build.
     Pros: small. Cons: hides the symptom for printtarg rows only; every
     other row stays wrong. Not recommended.
Recommendation: A.
Your decision needed: yes. Knut's prose says the screen wins, the rule
table under it says the block wins, and the section is unconfirmed. The
project rule is that a code/spec conflict is approved before it is
changed, and the spec text is Knut's. See Decision 2.

P2. Four default Guided charts are flagged red by the app's own instrument
    limits (F-018 corrected by R-018, R-101, N-4). OBSERVED. HIGH, but a
    design decision.
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Problem: In Guided, change nothing but instrument and paper, press
Generate, and the "Measured from Preview" frame shows red text saying the
margins are below the instrument minimum. This happens for: i1Pro 3 Plus
A4 landscape (left 26 < 28, top 33.4 < 40), i1Pro 3 Plus A4 portrait,
i1Pro A4 landscape (top 29.5 < 38), i1Pro Letter landscape. i1Pro A4
portrait and ColorMunki A4 pass.

What was tested: nine Guided default builds by the reviewer, four by
Agent 1, the code, and the tests.

What was observed: Guided does NOT apply the instrument-limits table at
all, on purpose: a shipped test pins that ("Guided behaves like before the
threshold feature"). The two combos that pass do so by arithmetic, not
because anything raised the margins. A method in the code promises a
Guided clamp but nothing calls it (dead code, R-101). Agent 1's first
explanation ("the clamp is overridden for landscape") was wrong.
One of these red sheets was already on record: the margin-inspector notes
(docs/dev_margin_inspector.md, issue #171) show a Guided i1Pro sheet with
top 27.2 < 38 and carry your ruling of 2026-08-26 that the Guided-versus-
Manual verdict difference is "known, deliberately not fixed, do not fix
without asking". That ruling was about the verdict flipping between the
two modules; whether Guided should meet the table at all was not ruled.
The seed rows use Knut's 11 mm run-up and the 19 mm bottom he asked for.

Why it matters: a first chart for an i1Pro 3 Plus owner arrives with red
warnings and Guided has no margin controls to fix it. The user's choices
are to ignore the warning or to go to Manual. The app contradicts itself.

Options:
  A. Guided honours the table for the chosen instrument and paper.
     Pros: no red default charts; the dead method is the natural hook.
     Cons: changes the geometry of every red Guided sheet (i1Pro and
     i1Pro 3 Plus landscape, and i1Pro 3 Plus A4 portrait: fewer strips),
     a shipped test must be inverted, and Guided will no longer match
     printtarg to the patch for those sheets (today it does).
  B. Review the seed rows (the landscape top 38/40 and the 3 Plus
     left 28 / right 9): if they are stricter than the jig needs, lower
     them. Pros: no geometry change. Cons: they are Knut's jig numbers
     (the 19 mm bottom row is a separate ruling and stays out of this).
  C. Show Guided's verdict as advisory ("Manual can meet the instrument
     limits") instead of red.
     Pros: honest, cheap. Cons: the red stays a warning nobody can act on
     in Guided.
  D. (considered in the inspector notes) Judge every sheet against
     whichever minimum is stricter and name which one failed.
     Pros: no geometry change, consistent verdicts. Cons: still red.
Recommendation: A for the instruments with a table, after the seed rows
are confirmed right (they are marked "editable starting points" in the
inspector notes). If in doubt about the seeds, B first, then A.
Your decision needed: yes. See Decision 1 (B is Knut's numbers).

P3. The layout estimate and the "last page not full" hint disagree with
    the chart the engine just built when the patch count is fixed
    (F-001, R-001). OBSERVED. HIGH.
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Problem: Manual, engine on, i1Pro A4, default "prioritise chart area"
layout, patch count fixed at 400. The build makes 418 patches on one full
page (the engine sizes the patches so that 400 fill the sheet). Right
after the build, the "estimate" column says 425, and during the build a
window said "there is space for about 107 more patches (the page holds
about 525)", although the page is full by construction.

What was tested: both agents, several fixed-count cases (400, 300, a
loaded 304-patch set), by-grid and patch-first. With "Auto" patch count
the estimate was exact in 66 of 66 builds. The mismatch appears only with
a fixed count or a loaded set.

Why it matters: these two frames are the engine's only feedback on
capacity. Three different numbers for one unchanged layout make them
untrustworthy, and the hint sends users to the patch-set editor to fill a
page that is already full.

Cause (INFERRED; reproduced offline by Agent 1, confirmed in the code by
Agent 2): the build tells the geometry how many patches there really are;
the estimate and the hint do not, so they assume a page filled with
default-size patches. The same file already does it right for the
helper-marker overlay, with a comment explaining this exact trap.

Options:
  A. Feed the designed count into the estimate and the hint the way the
     overlay already does. Pros: one known pattern; Auto-count estimates
     do not change (they pass no count); the 55-build matrix is the
     safety net. Cons: numbers users have seen for fixed counts will move.
  B. Also: suppress the hint for area-first charts, where the page is
     full by definition.
  C. Print the assumption behind the estimate when the columns differ.
Recommendation: A plus B.
Your decision needed: only on B (keep or drop the hint for area-first).
See Decision 11.

P4. A change in Preferences > Instrument Limits does not reach the layout

  B. Read the table lazily at estimate and build time instead of copying

P5. One visit to "From Profile Gamut" on a run without a profile disables
    Generate everywhere and shows a Stop button (F-024, F-023, N-5).
    OBSERVED. HIGH.
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Problem: Run type Verification on a run with no profile. Click FROM
PROFILE GAMUT: Generate greys out and STOP appears (nothing is running).
Click GUIDED or MANUAL: still grey. Run type back to Profiling: still
grey. The reviewer showed it survives run switches, run-type switches and
even opening another project. Only a gamut visit on a run WITH a profile
gives Generate back.

Why it matters: a user who looks at the gamut module once loses Generate
in every module, under an info box that says "You can go ahead and create
the chart", with a Stop button that suggests a stuck build.

Cause (code, both agents): the gamut module disables the button and
nothing re-enables it on a module or run change; and the app decides "a
build is running" by looking at whether the Generate button is disabled,
which is why Stop appears. Generate's enabled state is set from twelve
places in the code.

Options:
  A. One central rule that recomputes Generate's state on every module,
     run and run-type change. Pros: fixes the leak and future ones.
  B. Keep the gamut rule inside the module only. Pros: smallest.
  C. Remember "a build is running" explicitly instead of reading the
     button (the auto-update preview asks the same question, so it
     benefits too).
Recommendation: A plus C.
Your decision needed: no (engineering); it is in the approval block,
Decision 15.

P6. A failed build silently deletes the previous chart's export files and
    leaves the preview blank (F-025 corrected, R-102). OBSERVED. MEDIUM,
    data safety.
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Problem: A good chart is on screen. You try a layout that cannot fit (the
app lets you, see P8). The build fails, the chart files are put back, but
the three hand-off files in exports/ (-colours.txt, -i1profiler.txt,
-i1profiler.pxf) are gone, and the preview stays at "NO PREVIEW" until
you switch runs and back. Agent 1's claim that the frames kept a stale
"Margins: OK" was wrong (they show placeholders); the file loss is new.

Why it matters: the hand-off files are what a user copies to i1Profiler;
they vanish after an attempt that "changed nothing". They can be
re-created by a successful rebuild, so it is not permanent loss, but it
breaks the promise that a failed build puts everything back.

Options:
  A. Stash exports/ with the chart and restore it on failure (NOT cache/:
     cache/ holds the New-run seed block, which a build must consume).
  B. Delete exports/ only after the new chart has been written.
  Plus, in either case: reload the restored chart into the preview on
  the failure branch, as the Stop path already does.
Recommendation: A plus the preview reload.
Your decision needed: engineering, in the approval block (Decision 15);
say there if you would rather accept "derived, rebuilt next time".
